Fuel Stop Selection Strategy

1. Initial 500-Mile Free Range

The vehicle departs the origin with a full 50-gallon tank (10 MPG = 500 miles range) at $0 cost. If the entire route is ≤ 500 miles (such as New York → Boston at 213.5 mi), zero stops are scheduled and total fuel spend is $0.00.

2. Cluster Deduplication (0.5 Mile Tolerance)

Multiple highway candidate stations clustered around the same highway exit (within 0.5 miles along the route) are grouped together. The optimizer selects the single lowest retail price candidate in the cluster, preventing redundant stop-hopping.

3. Lookahead Strategy: Cheaper Station Ahead

From the current position, the vehicle looks ahead up to 500 miles. If a strictly cheaper station is reachable ahead, it purchases only enough fuel to reach that cheaper station, avoiding expensive overfill at the current station.

4. Regional Minimum Rule: Locking in Low Prices

If no cheaper station is reachable ahead within 500 miles, the current station represents the local price minimum across the horizon. The vehicle purchases fuel up to full tank capacity (or the exact gallons required to reach the destination) to maximize miles driven at the cheapest rate.

5. Physical Invariants & Feasibility

Tank capacity never exceeds 50.0 gallons and fuel never drops below 0 gallons. The destination is treated as a terminal node with $0 price—no unnecessary fuel is ever purchased once the destination is within range.
